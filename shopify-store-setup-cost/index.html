How much does a Shopify store setup cost?

Plan fees, themes, apps, design and launch work: the components of a Shopify budget, with rough ranges.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: A Shopify store can cost very little if you set it up yourself on a free theme, plus the monthly plan fee and payment processing. Hiring help ranges roughly from a few hundred dollars for a basic setup to several thousand for a designed store, and more for custom builds. Ongoing apps, themes and transaction fees add to the total.

"How much does a Shopify store cost?" has two answers: what Shopify charges you, and what it costs to make the store good enough to sell. The first is published and fairly predictable. The second depends on how much you do yourself and how custom you want the store to be. The ranges below are rough, illustrative and vary widely; always check Shopify's own pricing page for current plan prices, as they change and differ by country.

What does Shopify itself charge?

  • A monthly or annual plan. Shopify offers several tiers, from entry-level plans for new stores to higher tiers with more staff accounts, reporting and lower card rates. Annual billing is usually discounted. Shopify often runs trials or introductory offers for new stores.
  • Payment processing. Card payments through Shopify Payments carry a per-transaction rate that depends on your plan and country. If you use a third-party payment provider instead, Shopify may add its own transaction fee on top of that provider's charges.
  • Domain. A custom domain costs a modest yearly fee, whether bought through Shopify or a registrar.

What do the main budget levels look like?

Do it yourself

A founder with a small catalogue can launch on a free theme with the entry plan, a domain and a few free apps. The cash cost is low; the real cost is time, often several days to a few weeks of evenings to get products, shipping, taxes, policies and pages right. This is a sensible way to validate demand before spending more.

Freelancer or small agency setup

Hiring help to configure a premium theme, load products, set up shipping and payments, and polish the main pages typically costs from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, depending on catalogue size and how much design is involved. You get a faster, cleaner launch and fewer setup mistakes.

Designed or custom store

A store with a custom design, bespoke sections, integrations with stock or fulfilment systems, or complex product options can cost several thousand to tens of thousands of dollars. This suits established brands where the store's look and conversion rate move meaningful revenue.

What ongoing costs should you plan for?

  • The monthly Shopify plan.
  • Card processing on every sale.
  • App subscriptions: email, reviews, subscriptions, search, upsells. Review these every few months; unused apps are a common leak and can also slow the store.
  • Theme updates and occasional developer help.
  • Marketing: ads, email, content and SEO, which usually costs more over a year than the build itself.

Where do small stores waste money?

  • Too many apps at launch. Start with the essentials and add only when a real problem appears.
  • Custom design before product-market fit. A premium theme done well is enough for most new stores.
  • Skipping basics: clear product pages, shipping information and policies matter more than animations. See our guide to Shopify product pages.
  • Ignoring search from day one. Clean titles, descriptions and URLs cost little at setup and are tedious to fix later; our Shopify SEO guide explains the basics.

How do you estimate your own budget?

A practical way to budget is to list each component in the table above and mark whether you will do it yourself, buy it or hire for it. Price the items you will buy from current published rates, ask two or three providers to quote the items you will hire for, and add the monthly costs for twelve months to see the first-year total. Then add a contingency, since most launches surface a few unplanned needs, such as an extra app, a shipping rule for a new region or a quick round of developer fixes.

Compare that first-year figure with what you expect the store to earn. A new store testing demand should keep build costs small and leave most of the budget for marketing, because a beautiful store with no traffic sells nothing. An established business moving from another platform can justify spending more on design and migration, since it already has customers and revenue to protect.

Is Shopify the right platform at all?

Shopify is popular with small retailers because it handles hosting, security, checkout and payments in one place. Alternatives include WooCommerce on WordPress, Squarespace, Wix and BigCommerce, each with different trade-offs between cost, control and ease of use. Compare total costs, including apps and payment fees, rather than plan prices alone.

What should you ask before hiring someone to build it?

  1. Will the store and all apps be registered to my account?
  2. Which theme will you use, and who pays for its licence?
  3. How many products, pages and revision rounds are included?
  4. Will you set up shipping, taxes, payments and legal policies?
  5. What training or handover do I get so I can run it myself?
  6. What ongoing app costs will I be left with?

Questions readers ask

Can I set up a Shopify store for free?

Not entirely. You can use a free theme and free apps, but you still pay a Shopify plan after any trial, plus payment processing and usually a domain.

How much does it cost to hire someone to set up Shopify?

Roughly a few hundred to a few thousand dollars for a standard setup, more for custom design or integrations. Ranges vary widely.

Do Shopify apps cost extra?

Many have free tiers, but paid apps are monthly subscriptions that can add up. Review them regularly.

Should a new store pay for a custom theme?

Usually not at first. A well-configured premium or free theme is enough until the store has proven demand.

Where can I check current Shopify plan prices?

On Shopify's own pricing page, which shows current plans and rates for your country.
