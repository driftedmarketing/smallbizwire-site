Profile: Housefly, a private AI assistant you text

A memory-first assistant from Drifted Marketing, offered as ten founding seats. What it does and what it will not do.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Housefly is a texted AI assistant made by Drifted Marketing, SmallBizWire's publisher. According to its site, it keeps a sealed memory for each member, tracks promises, debts and call briefs, reads inbox, store and ad numbers, and asks before anything is sent, paid or changed. Concierge tiers are listed at $3,000 or $6,000 a quarter.

Housefly is an AI assistant made by Drifted Marketing, SmallBizWire's publisher. Its paid offering, Housefly Concierge, is being opened to ten founding members. This profile is based on Housefly's published site.

The idea

Most assistants wait to be opened. Housefly is designed to be texted like a person, iMessage first, and to learn the member's memory and taste from those conversations. It also speaks first: when something needs attention before a call or a deadline, it sends a message rather than waiting to be asked.

What it keeps track of

  • What the member has promised clients.
  • What clients still owe the member.
  • A brief before each call.
  • The member's inbox, store and ad numbers, read.

What it will not do on its own

Anything that sends, pays or changes an account is named in a single sentence, and then Housefly waits for the member's yes. This "drafts, you approve" pattern is the same one we recommend for every assistant in our guide.

Sealed memory

Each member's memory is kept separately. According to its site, no other member and no shared brain sees inside it. The service carries no ads, and the site states that member data is not for sale; the business model is the member's subscription.

When it hits a wall

For jobs an assistant cannot finish, such as design, video and outreach, Drifted Marketing's own team picks them up.

Price and availability

The site lists two tiers, Solo at $3,000 a quarter and Operator at $6,000 a quarter, paid up front each quarter, with founding members keeping their price for life in exchange for a short weekly feedback call. There are ten founding seats. The site notes that the difference between the two tiers is still to be defined, and that there are not yet member testimonials to share.

Who it may suit

Founders and operators juggling many clients who want an assistant that remembers and reminds, and who place a high value on privacy. Owners who want a low-cost general tool will find the mainstream assistants in our comparison a better fit.

What questions should I ask any private AI assistant?

  1. Where is my memory stored, and who can see it? Ask for the answer in writing, including whether staff can access it and under what circumstances.
  2. Which model providers process my messages? Many assistants are built on third-party models; ask which, and what those providers' data terms are.
  3. What can it do without asking? Ask for the exact list of actions that need your approval.
  4. How do I export or delete my data? Ask what happens to your memory if you cancel.
  5. What does the price include? Ask what separates any tiers and whether human help is included or extra.

Is an expensive assistant worth it for a small business?

It depends on what a missed promise or a forgotten follow-up costs you. For an owner with a handful of customers, a low-cost general chatbot and a good calendar may be enough. For someone managing many clients, the value lies in memory and reminders that actually arrive. Work out roughly what your time and your clients are worth before comparing prices, and run any trial on real but non-sensitive work first.

What does "drafts, you approve" look like in practice?

An assistant prepares a reply, a payment or a change, describes it in one sentence, and waits. You read the sentence and say yes or no. Nothing leaves without that step. This pattern is the core recommendation of our guide to how small businesses should actually use AI assistants, and it applies whatever tool you choose.

A checklist before signing up to any assistant

  • Read the privacy policy and data terms in full.
  • Confirm which actions require your approval.
  • Confirm how to export and delete your data.
  • Understand the billing period and cancellation terms.
  • Decide in advance how you will judge it after the first month.

How do I judge an assistant after the first month?

Before you start, write down two or three things you want it to do better than your current system, such as catching follow-ups you would have forgotten or preparing you for calls. After a month, check each one honestly against real examples. Also note anything it got wrong or any moment it acted in a way you did not expect. Those notes, not the sign-up page, are the best basis for deciding whether to renew.
