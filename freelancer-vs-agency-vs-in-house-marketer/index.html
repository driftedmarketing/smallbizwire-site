Freelancer vs agency vs in-house marketer: which should a small business hire?

A side-by-side comparison of cost, speed, skills, control and risk, and when each one makes sense.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: A freelancer suits one well-defined skill on a small budget. An agency suits several channels at once, with a team and process behind it. An in-house marketer suits a business with steady, ongoing work and the budget for a salary. Many small businesses combine them, for example one in-house generalist plus a specialist freelancer or agency.

The question is usually framed as which option is best. It is more useful to ask which shape of help fits your stage, budget and the work you actually need done. A business that needs one excellent ad buyer has a different answer from one that needs a brand, a website, weekly content and paid media at once.

What does each option really mean?

A freelancer is one independent professional, usually strong in one discipline: ads, design, copywriting, video editing, SEO. You hire their skill and their time.

An agency is a team, from a handful of people to hundreds, with defined roles, process and usually a wider range of skills. You hire an outcome delivered by several people.

An in-house marketer is an employee, full or part-time, who works only for you. You hire their attention and their growing knowledge of your business, along with the cost and responsibility of employing someone.

When does a freelancer make sense?

Hire a freelancer when the work is a single, well-defined skill and you can brief it clearly. Examples: someone to run your Meta ads at a modest budget, a designer for a set of product images, an editor who cuts weekly short videos from footage you shoot, or a copywriter for a new website. Freelancers are often the most cost-effective way to get senior skill in one area.

The trade-offs are capacity and continuity. A good freelancer is busy, and if they fall ill, take a big client or move on, the work stops. Keep every account and file in your own name so that switching is painless.

When does an agency make sense?

Hire an agency when you need several disciplines working together, such as creative that feeds ads that feed a landing page, and you do not want to coordinate three freelancers yourself. Agencies also bring process: briefs, calendars, reporting, approvals and cover when someone is away.

The trade-offs are cost and the risk that your account is handed to junior people after the senior team wins the deal. Ask exactly who will work on your account and meet them. Our agency pricing guide explains how fees are built, and our selection guide covers the questions to ask.

When does an in-house marketer make sense?

Hire in-house when marketing work is steady, daily and closely tied to your product, customers and voice, and when the business can afford a salary with confidence for the next year or more. An in-house person learns your customers in a way an outsider rarely does, and can react the same day.

The trade-off is that one person cannot be expert in everything. A common mistake is hiring a single junior marketer and expecting them to be strategist, designer, ad buyer, copywriter and analyst at once. That role usually works best as a coordinator who owns the plan and brings in specialists.

What about combining them?

For many small businesses the best answer is a mix:

  • Owner plus freelancer: the owner sets direction and approves; a freelancer runs one channel.
  • In-house generalist plus specialists: one employee owns the calendar and brand voice and briefs freelancers or an agency for ads, video or SEO.
  • Agency for build, in-house for running: an agency builds the website or launch campaign; an employee keeps it going.

How do you decide?

  1. List the work you need each month, by skill.
  2. Count the skills. One skill points to a freelancer; several at once point to an agency; steady daily work points to in-house.
  3. Check the budget honestly, including management time, which is a real cost.
  4. Decide how much control you need over day-to-day decisions.
  5. Start small and reversible. A three-month freelancer or agency engagement teaches you what you need before you commit to a hire.

How do you brief whichever option you choose?

The quality of outside help depends heavily on the brief. Whether you hire a freelancer, an agency or an employee, write down your best customers and why they buy, your margins on the main products, what has been tried before and what happened, the one measure you care about this quarter, and the decisions you want to keep for yourself. Share examples of work you like and dislike. A one-page brief written in an hour saves weeks of misdirected effort and makes it easier to judge, after three months, whether the hire worked.

How do you measure whether the hire worked?

Agree the measure before the work starts, and review it on a fixed date. For paid media that might be cost per order or return on ad spend; for content, qualified enquiries or email sign-ups; for SEO, visits from non-brand searches over a longer period. Compare against the cost of the help, including your own management time. If the numbers have not moved and nobody can explain why, change the brief, the scope or the person rather than simply spending more.

Does location change the answer?

It can. Freelancers and agencies abroad can lower costs, and time zones can even work in your favour, but they add questions about communication, payment and contracts. We cover those in our guide to offshore agencies.

Questions readers ask

Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency?

Usually for a single skill, yes, because you are not paying for a team and overhead. For several skills at once, coordinating multiple freelancers can cost more time than it saves.

When should a small business hire a full-time marketer?

When marketing work is steady and daily, and the business can confidently afford a salary for at least a year.

Can one in-house marketer do everything?

Rarely well. The role works best as an owner of the plan who briefs specialists for ads, design, video or SEO.

What is the biggest risk with a freelancer?

Being a single point of failure. Keep accounts and files in your own name so you can switch easily.

Can I combine an agency with an in-house hire?

Yes, and many businesses do: an in-house generalist coordinates while an agency or freelancers handle specialist work.
