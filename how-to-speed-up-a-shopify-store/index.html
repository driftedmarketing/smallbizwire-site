How do I speed up a Shopify store?

Most slow Shopify stores are slow for the same three reasons: too many apps, oversized images and a heavy theme.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Start by removing apps you no longer use, because many inject scripts on every page even after you stop using them. Then compress and correctly size your images, keep video off the top of the homepage, and choose a lean, well-maintained theme. Measure before and after with Google PageSpeed Insights so you know what actually helped.

A slow store costs you in two places. Shoppers on a phone with a weak signal give up before the page finishes loading, and search engines treat page experience as one of many signals when deciding what to show. The good news is that Shopify handles the hard infrastructure for you: hosting, a content delivery network and SSL are included on every plan. What is left in your hands is what you put on the page, and that is where nearly all of the slowness comes from.

This guide walks through the fixes in the order that usually gives the most improvement for the least effort. Exact gains vary from store to store, so treat any number you read online, including the rough ranges here, as a hint rather than a promise. The only number that matters is the one you measure on your own store.

How do I measure my store's speed first?

Before changing anything, take a baseline. Three free tools cover most needs:

  • Google PageSpeed Insights. Paste in your homepage, a collection page and a product page separately. It reports lab results and, when your store has enough traffic, real-user data from the Chrome User Experience Report.
  • Shopify's own web performance report. In the admin, under Online Store or Analytics depending on your version, Shopify shows Core Web Vitals for your store. It is based on real visits rather than a single test.
  • Your own phone. Load the store on mobile data, not office wifi. If it feels slow to you, it feels slow to customers.

Write the results down. Test the same three pages after each change, because a fix that helps the homepage can do nothing for product pages, and the reverse.

Which Core Web Vitals should I care about?

Google's Core Web Vitals are three measurements of how a page feels to use:

MetricWhat it measuresUsual culprit on Shopify
Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)How long until the main content, often the hero image, appearsA huge hero image or an autoplaying video banner
Interaction to Next Paint (INP)How quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicksToo much JavaScript from apps and tracking scripts
Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)How much the layout jumps around while loadingPop-ups, banners and images without set dimensions

Google publishes target thresholds for each one in its documentation. You do not need to memorise them; PageSpeed Insights colours each result green, amber or red for you.

Why do apps slow a Shopify store down?

Apps are the single most common cause of a slow Shopify store. Each app that adds something to your storefront, such as reviews, upsells, chat widgets, currency switchers or pop-ups, usually loads its own JavaScript and sometimes its own stylesheet. One app is rarely the problem. Fifteen apps, each loading on every page, add up quickly.

There is a second, quieter problem. On older themes, some apps edited your theme code directly when you installed them. Uninstalling the app did not always remove that code, so the store keeps trying to load files from a service you no longer pay for. Newer apps built on Shopify's theme app extensions are easier to remove cleanly, but leftovers from years past are still common.

A practical app audit:

  1. List every installed app and write down what it does and whether anyone still uses it.
  2. Uninstall anything unused. Then check the app's documentation for any manual cleanup steps.
  3. Look for overlap. Two apps doing reviews, or three doing pop-ups, is a common find.
  4. Ask whether a built-in feature now covers the job. Shopify has added native features over the years that replaced the need for some apps.
  5. Retest the same three pages.

If you are comfortable in the theme editor, you can also search the theme code for script tags pointing to apps you have removed. If you are not, a developer can do this in an hour or two.

How should I handle product and banner images?

Images are usually the heaviest thing on a store page. Shopify's content delivery network automatically serves resized and modern-format versions of images to supporting browsers, which helps a great deal, but it cannot fix every habit.

  • Do not upload enormous originals. A photo straight from a modern camera can be several thousand pixels wide and many megabytes. Resize to a sensible width for your theme before uploading. Your theme documentation usually recommends sizes.
  • Compress before upload. Free tools can cut file size noticeably with little visible loss. Do it once, at the source.
  • Keep the hero simple. A single well-compressed image loads faster than a slideshow of five. Slideshows also tend to hurt layout shift and are often ignored by shoppers anyway.
  • Be careful with video at the top. An autoplaying background video is one of the most expensive things you can put above the fold. If you want video, consider placing it lower on the page or showing a still image with a play button.
  • Write alt text. It does not affect speed, but while you are in there it helps accessibility and image search. Our guide to Shopify product page SEO covers it in more detail.

Does my Shopify theme affect speed?

Yes, and more than many owners expect. Shopify's Online Store 2.0 themes, including its free default themes, are generally built with performance in mind. Some third-party themes pack in dozens of features, animations and sections, and every one of them carries code even if you do not use it.

Things to check in your theme:

  • Is it maintained? A theme that has not been updated in years may not use current performance techniques.
  • How many sections are on the homepage? Every section is more images and more code. Remove the ones that do not earn their place.
  • Are you using custom fonts? Each font weight is a separate download. Two weights of one family is usually plenty.
  • Is the theme heavily customised? Years of edits by different developers can leave duplicated or dead code behind.

Switching themes is a bigger project than an app audit, so do it last, and only if measurements still point to the theme after you have cleaned up apps and images. Test a new theme on an unpublished copy first.

What about tracking pixels and third-party scripts?

Analytics, advertising pixels and heatmap tools all add JavaScript. Shopify's customer events and its built-in integrations for major ad platforms can reduce the need to paste raw code into your theme. Review what is installed, remove duplicates (the same pixel installed twice is common), and keep only the tools someone actually looks at.

What speed fixes are not worth chasing?

Some advice is wasted effort on Shopify. You cannot change the hosting, so offers to "move to faster servers" do not apply. Chasing a perfect score of 100 is rarely worth the cost; a store that feels fast on a phone and passes Core Web Vitals is in good shape. Be wary of apps that promise to speed up your store by adding yet another script; read reviews carefully and measure before and after, because some help and some simply add weight.

What order should I do all this in?

  1. Measure three page types and write the results down.
  2. Audit and remove unused or duplicate apps.
  3. Fix the hero area: one compressed image, no autoplaying video.
  4. Resize and compress product and collection images going forward.
  5. Remove duplicate pixels and unused scripts.
  6. Trim homepage sections and font weights.
  7. Only then consider a theme change.

Speed is also one part of a wider picture. Once the store is quick, our Shopify SEO guide covers the content and structure that help it get found, and the anatomy of a landing page covers what to put on a fast page so it actually sells.

Recheck speed every few months, and every time you install a new app. Stores rarely get slow in one day; they get slow one small addition at a time.

Questions readers ask

Does Shopify host my store on fast servers?

Yes. Hosting and a global content delivery network are included on every Shopify plan, so speed problems almost always come from what is on the page rather than the server.

How many apps is too many?

There is no fixed number. What matters is how many load code on your storefront and whether each one is still used. Audit them and measure before and after removing any.

Will a speed optimisation app fix my store?

Sometimes, but some add their own scripts. Measure your pages before and after installing one and keep it only if the numbers improve.

Do I need a perfect PageSpeed score?

No. Aim for passing Core Web Vitals and a store that feels quick on a phone. A perfect score is rarely worth the cost.

Should I switch themes to get faster?

Only after removing unused apps and fixing images. If measurements still point to the theme, test a lean, maintained theme on an unpublished copy first.
