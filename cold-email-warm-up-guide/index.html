Cold email warm-up: how to build sender reputation without burning your domain

A new mailbox that sends hundreds of emails on day one looks like spam. Warm-up is the slow, boring fix.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Warm up a new cold-email domain by sending small, slowly rising volumes of real, wanted mail for several weeks before outreach. Use a separate domain from your main one, authenticate it with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, start with a handful of messages a day, increase gradually, and pause the moment bounces or spam complaints rise.

Mailbox providers judge senders on history. A brand-new address with no track record that suddenly sends a large batch of messages to strangers matches the pattern of a spammer, and gets treated like one. Warm-up means building that history gradually.

Protect your main domain first

Many businesses run cold outreach from a separate, similar domain (for example, a "get" or "hello" variant of the brand name) rather than the domain used for invoices and customer support. If outreach damages that domain's reputation, your everyday mail keeps flowing. The secondary domain should still be honest about who you are, carry its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC records, and ideally redirect visitors to your main website.

A sensible ramp

There is no official schedule, but practitioners broadly agree on the shape:

  • Weeks one and two: send only genuine, low-volume mail. A handful a day to people likely to reply: colleagues, partners, existing contacts.
  • Weeks three and four: begin small cold batches, increasing gradually, and watch bounces and replies closely.
  • After that: hold a steady daily volume per mailbox rather than spiking. If you need more volume, add mailboxes rather than pushing one harder.

Automated warm-up services, which exchange messages between networks of inboxes, are common. They can help, but they are no substitute for real replies from real people, which are the strongest positive signal you can earn.

What hurts reputation fastest

  • Bounces. Verify every list before sending. Purchased or scraped lists are full of dead and trap addresses.
  • Spam complaints. Google advises keeping reported spam rates below 0.1% and never letting them reach 0.3%.
  • Identical messages. Hundreds of word-for-word copies look automated. Write for a specific segment and reference something real about the recipient.
  • Heavy links and images. A first-touch email works best as plain text with one link at most.

Measure the right thing

Open rates are increasingly unreliable because of privacy features that pre-load images. Track replies, positive replies and bounces instead. Check Google Postmaster Tools, which is free, for your domain's reputation once volume is high enough to register.

Stay on the right side of the law

Rules differ by country. In the United States, CAN-SPAM requires accurate sender details, a physical postal address and a working opt-out. In the UK and EU, rules on unsolicited email are stricter, particularly for individuals rather than companies. If you sell across borders, check the rules where your recipients are.

Before you start, make sure your authentication records are in place, then work through the full checklist.

What does a sensible warm-up schedule look like?

There is no official schedule, and mailbox providers do not publish thresholds, so treat any plan as a cautious guide rather than a rule. The principle is gradual growth with no sudden jumps. A conservative pattern for a single new inbox might look like this:

PeriodDaily sends per inboxWhat to send
Week 1Very low, single digitsReal conversations with colleagues, partners and people who will reply
Week 2Low, rising slowlyMore real conversations; replies to inbound messages
Weeks 3–4ModerateFirst small batches of carefully targeted outreach
After week 4Hold steadyScale by adding inboxes, not by pushing one inbox harder

How do I set up a domain for cold email?

  1. Register a domain close to your main brand, so prospects recognise it.
  2. Point its website to your main site, so it does not look abandoned.
  3. Create one or a few named inboxes with a real person's name, photo and signature.
  4. Publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC, as explained in our plain-English authentication guide.
  5. Wait a little before sending; brand-new domains are often treated with suspicion.

What signals tell me warm-up is going wrong?

  • Bounces climbing. Usually a list problem. Verify addresses before sending and remove anything that bounces.
  • Spam complaints. Google's 2024 bulk sender guidance asks senders to keep reported spam rates low, and below 0.3% at the very most. Cold mail should aim far lower.
  • Replies drying up. If people who replied before stop replying, your mail may be landing in spam.
  • Postmaster data. Google Postmaster Tools can show domain reputation once you send enough volume to Gmail.

When any of these move the wrong way, cut volume, fix the cause, and only then resume.

Do automated warm-up tools help?

Automated warm-up networks send messages between participating inboxes and mark them as important. Opinions differ on how much providers credit this activity, and some providers' terms frown on artificial engagement. They are not a substitute for a clean list, relevant messages and patient volume. If you use one, treat it as a supplement and keep sending real mail too.

How do I keep a domain healthy after warm-up?

  • Keep daily volume per inbox steady rather than spiky.
  • Write short, plain-text messages that refer to something specific about the recipient.
  • Include a simple way to opt out, and honour it immediately.
  • Stop sequences when someone replies.
  • Rest a domain if its numbers deteriorate, instead of pushing through.

Before each campaign, run through the deliverability checklist. Most damage happens in the first campaign after warm-up, when excitement leads to sending too much, too fast.

What does a good warm-up message look like?

The best warm-up mail is mail you would send anyway. Reply to suppliers from the new inbox, send meeting confirmations, introduce yourself to partners and ask colleagues to reply with a real sentence rather than a one-word acknowledgement. Conversations with back-and-forth replies are a stronger positive signal than one-way announcements. Avoid sending the same template to dozens of friendly contacts, which looks like the bulk pattern you are trying to avoid. Keep a short note of what you sent each day and how many replies arrived, so that when outreach begins you have a baseline to compare against. If the baseline was healthy and outreach numbers fall, the change in behaviour is the likely cause.

Questions readers ask

How long does email warm-up take?

Most practitioners allow three to four weeks of gradually increasing volume before sending at full pace, and keep volume steady afterwards.

Should I send cold email from my main domain?

Many businesses use a separate, clearly related domain for outreach so their everyday mail is protected if reputation suffers.

Do warm-up tools work?

They can help build history, but genuine replies from real recipients are a stronger signal and cannot be replaced.

How many cold emails can I send per day per inbox?

No provider publishes a safe number. Keep per-inbox volume modest and steady, and grow by adding inboxes and domains rather than pushing one inbox harder.

What should I do if my domain lands in spam?

Pause outreach, check authentication, clean the list, and send only real conversations at low volume until replies and inbox placement recover.
