How do I write Shopify collection pages that rank?

Collection pages often target the search terms with the most buyers. Most stores leave them as a bare grid of products.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Treat each collection as a landing page for one clear search term. Give it a descriptive title and handle, a short intro above the products that tells shoppers what is in the collection, helpful copy below, and a unique meta description. Link to it from navigation and related products, and avoid near-duplicate collections competing for the same term.

When someone searches "linen bath towels" or "women's leather ankle boots," they usually want to see a range, not a single product. That is exactly what a collection page is. In many stores, collection pages are the pages best matched to the searches with the most buying intent, yet they are often the thinnest pages on the site: a heading, a grid of thumbnails and nothing else.

This guide covers how to turn a Shopify collection into a page that search engines understand and shoppers find useful. It complements our guide to Shopify product page SEO, which handles the individual product level.

What makes a collection page rank?

Search engines are trying to show the page that best answers a query. For a collection, that means three things:

  • Clear relevance. The page is plainly about one kind of product, and its title, heading and copy say so.
  • Usefulness. It helps a shopper choose: a good range, sensible filters, and a little guidance.
  • Signals of importance. Your own site links to it prominently, and ideally other sites link to it too.

None of this is a trick. It is the same thing a good shop assistant does: put the right products together, label the shelf, and point people to it.

How do I choose the search term for each collection?

Each collection should target one main term and its close variations. Start by listing how customers actually describe the products. Use your own site search data, questions customers ask, and the suggestions that appear as you type into Google. Free and paid keyword tools can give rough search volumes, but treat them as estimates.

A good collection term is specific enough to match intent and broad enough to justify a range. "Towels" may be too broad to win; "cotton bath towels" is a clearer target; "blue cotton bath towel 70x140" is probably a single product.

Write the plan down in a simple table:

CollectionMain search termClose variations
Cotton bath towelscotton bath towels100% cotton towels, soft bath towels
Kids' bath towelskids hooded towelschildren's bath towels, toddler towels
Beach towelsbeach towelslarge beach towels, sand-free towels

If two collections want the same term, merge them or make their focus clearly different. Two near-identical collections split your effort and can confuse search engines about which one to show.

What should go in the title, handle and meta description?

  • Collection title. This becomes the page heading in most themes. Use the plain name customers search for: "Cotton Bath Towels," not "The Cloud Edit."
  • SEO title. Set in the collection's search engine listing section. Lead with the term, then the brand: "Cotton Bath Towels | Your Store Name." Keep it short enough that it is not cut off in results.
  • Handle (URL). Shopify collection URLs follow the pattern /collections/handle. Keep the handle short and descriptive: cotton-bath-towels. If you change an existing handle, make sure a redirect from the old URL is in place; Shopify offers to create one when you edit it.
  • Meta description. One or two sentences that say what is in the collection and why to click. Google may rewrite it, but a good one often gets used.

How much text should a collection page have?

Enough to help, not so much that it buries the products. A pattern that works on most themes:

  1. A short intro above the grid. Two to four sentences that confirm what the collection is and who it is for. Shoppers should see products without scrolling far.
  2. The product grid, with filters that match how people choose: size, colour, material, price.
  3. Longer, useful copy below the grid. How to choose between options, care instructions, sizing guidance, and answers to common questions. Many themes support a section below products; if yours does not, a developer can add one using a metafield.

Write for a shopper, not for a search engine. Repeating the search term in every sentence reads badly and does not help. Mention it naturally in the heading, the intro and once or twice below.

How do I avoid duplicate content on collections?

Shopify can produce several URLs for the same product, such as a product URL nested under a collection path. Shopify's themes generally set a canonical tag pointing to the main product URL, which tells search engines which version to treat as the original. Check that your theme does this; most current themes do.

Filtered and sorted views of collections can also generate many URL variations. Shopify's native filtering is generally handled sensibly, but if you use a third-party filter app, check how it creates URLs and whether those pages are being indexed. Google Search Console's page indexing report is the place to look.

Finally, avoid copying the same intro text across many collections. Each collection should have its own words.

How should I link to my collections?

Internal links tell search engines which pages you consider important. Your most valuable collections should be:

  • In the main navigation or a clear sub-menu.
  • Linked from the homepage, where it makes sense.
  • Linked from related product pages, for example "See all cotton bath towels."
  • Linked from blog posts or guides on related topics, using descriptive anchor text rather than "click here."

Breadcrumbs, which many themes include, also help both shoppers and search engines understand where a page sits.

Should I add structured data to collection pages?

Structured data helps search engines read a page's content. On collection pages, breadcrumb markup is the most widely useful. Product markup belongs on individual product pages. Many themes include basic structured data already; check with Google's Rich Results Test before adding more, so you do not duplicate it.

How do I keep collections working over time?

  • Keep them stocked. A collection full of sold-out products is a poor result for a shopper. Consider sorting in-stock items first.
  • Do not delete seasonal collections every year. A collection that ranks for "Christmas gift sets" can be refreshed and reused rather than rebuilt from scratch, keeping its history.
  • Review in Search Console. See which collections appear for which searches, and whether click-through is low (a sign the title or description could be clearer).
  • Keep the page fast. Large grids of big images slow things down. Our guide on speeding up a Shopify store covers the fixes.

Collection pages are rarely glamorous work. They are also some of the most reliable pages a small store can improve, because the demand already exists; the page just needs to answer it clearly. For the wider picture of structure and content across your store, see our Shopify SEO guide.

Questions readers ask

Should collection pages have text?

Yes. A short intro above the products and helpful copy below helps shoppers choose and helps search engines understand what the page is about.

Can I change a collection's URL handle?

Yes, but set up a redirect from the old URL so you keep any rankings and links. Shopify offers to create one when you edit the handle.

Do filtered collection URLs cause duplicate content?

They can, depending on the filter app. Check how it builds URLs and review Google Search Console's page indexing report for unwanted pages.

How long should a collection intro be?

Two to four sentences above the grid is usually enough. Put longer guidance below the products so shoppers see items first.

Should two collections target the same keyword?

No. Merge them or give each a clearly different focus, so they do not compete with each other.
