How do US small businesses work with agencies in another country?

A practical setup guide: overlap hours, payments, a written scope and keeping every account in your name.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Agree a daily overlap window and a weekly call, put the scope and fees in writing, pay invoices in an agreed currency through a documented method, and keep every ad account, domain, store and file owned by your business with the agency added as a user. Then run the relationship through written briefs and a shared task list.

Working with an agency in another country is no longer unusual. The tools are the same, video calls are free and most marketing platforms let you add outside partners to your accounts. What makes or breaks the relationship is the setup in the first two weeks. This guide is a practical checklist for a US small business, whether the agency is in Pakistan, India, the Philippines, Eastern Europe, Latin America or anywhere else.

Step 1: How do you find working-hour overlap?

Start by writing down both time zones and when each side is actually available. A free world-clock tool shows the gap, but remember that daylight saving changes on different dates in different countries, and some countries do not change at all, so the gap can shift during the year.

  • Define an overlap window. Even one or two hours a day when both sides are online makes quick questions possible.
  • Fix a weekly call inside that window, with an agenda, so decisions are not left to chat.
  • Agree response times: for example, replies within one working day, and a separate, named route for urgent problems such as a broken checkout or a disapproved ad.
  • Ask whether the agency shifts its hours to match yours. Some deliberately work US hours; others offer a short overlap and do the bulk of the work while you sleep.

Time difference can be an advantage. Feedback sent at the end of your day can be done by the next morning, if briefs are clear enough that no clarifying question is needed.

Step 2: How do you write the scope down?

Distance punishes vagueness. Before work starts, agree in writing:

  • The deliverables per month or per project, with numbers.
  • What you will supply and by when: product details, photos, approvals, logins.
  • How many revision rounds are included.
  • The one or two measures that define success and the date you will review them.
  • Who the named contact is on each side.

Step 3: How should payments be handled?

DecisionWhat to agree
CurrencyUsually US dollars for US clients, so exchange-rate risk sits with the agency
MethodInternational wire or a payment service that supports the agency's country; confirm it works before the first invoice
FeesWho pays transfer and conversion fees, and whether the invoice amount is sent or received
TimingPayment terms (for example, due on receipt or within 14 days), allowing extra days for transfers
ProjectsMilestone payments tied to delivered work rather than the full amount up front
Ad spendPaid by you directly to the ad platform, never sent to the agency

Ask your accountant what records you need for payments to a foreign contractor. Requirements depend on your business structure, and it is easier to collect the right paperwork at the start than at tax time.

Step 4: Why must every account stay in your name?

This is the single most important rule. If the agency owns your ad account, domain or store, ending the relationship can mean losing your history, your audiences and even your website. Across borders, recovering them is harder still.

  • Meta: create the Business portfolio and ad account yourself, then add the agency as a partner.
  • Google Ads and Analytics: create them under your own Google account and grant the agency access.
  • Shopify or website: you own the store or hosting and add the agency as a staff or collaborator account.
  • Domain: registered to your business, with the renewal on your card.
  • Files: a shared drive you own, with source files delivered as work is completed.
  • Passwords: never share your main login. Use the platform's own user and partner access, and a password manager for anything else.

Step 5: How do you run the work day to day?

  1. One shared task list. A project board or a shared document that shows what is in progress, waiting on you, and done.
  2. Written briefs. Each piece of work starts with a short brief: goal, audience, format, deadline, examples you like.
  3. Recorded feedback. A short screen recording explaining changes often beats a long email across time zones.
  4. A monthly report against the agreed measures, not a pile of vanity numbers.
  5. A quarterly review to decide whether to continue, change scope or stop.

How do you handle language and market context?

Even a fluent English-speaking team may not know your local customers, idioms, holidays or advertising rules. Share examples of your best customer messages, a short brand voice note, your key dates and any regulated claims you must avoid. Approve customer-facing copy yourself, at least for the first months.

What should the first two weeks look like?

A simple onboarding plan prevents most early friction. In week one, sign the contract, set up access through each platform's partner tools, share the brief and brand notes, and hold a kick-off call to agree the task list. In week two, review the agency's first small deliverable, give detailed feedback, and confirm the reporting format. Small early deliverables reveal misunderstandings while they are cheap to fix, and they show you how the team responds to feedback before larger work depends on it.

Keep a short running log of decisions and changes in the shared task list, so anyone joining later on either side can see why things were done.

What are the common mistakes to avoid?

  • Sending ad budgets abroad instead of paying platforms directly.
  • Letting the agency create accounts in its own name "to save time."
  • Relying on chat messages instead of a written scope.
  • Expecting instant replies at 3 am the agency's time without agreeing it.
  • Paying an entire project fee up front.

Questions readers ask

How much time-zone overlap do I need with an overseas agency?

Even one or two hours a day plus a weekly scheduled call is workable if briefs and feedback are written clearly.

Should I send my ad budget to the agency?

No. Pay the ad platforms directly from an account in your business's name and give the agency access.

What currency should I pay in?

Most overseas agencies serving US clients invoice in US dollars. Agree the currency and who pays transfer fees in writing.

How do I give an overseas agency access safely?

Use each platform's own partner or user access rather than sharing your main login, and keep ownership with your business.

Do I need special tax paperwork to pay a foreign agency?

Possibly. Requirements depend on your business, so ask your accountant before the first payment.
