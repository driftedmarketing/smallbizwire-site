Local SEO checklist for service-area businesses

Plumbers, cleaners, electricians and mobile services play by slightly different local SEO rules. Here is the checklist.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: A service-area business serves customers at their location rather than its own. Set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business, hide your address if customers do not visit, and list the areas you really serve. Then keep your details consistent, build a page for each main service, earn reviews from every job, and avoid fake addresses.

A service-area business is one that goes to its customers rather than having customers come to it. Plumbers, electricians, cleaners, mobile mechanics, pest control, gardeners, dog walkers, mobile hairdressers and many consultants fall into this group. Some have a shop or office customers can visit as well; many work from home or a yard.

Google treats these businesses a little differently in its Business Profile guidelines, and the differences matter. Get them wrong and a profile can be suspended. Get them right and you can compete in local results across the area you actually serve. This checklist covers the essentials in order. Google updates its guidelines from time to time, so read the current version of its Business Profile guidelines before making changes.

Is my business a service-area business?

SituationHow Google treats itShow your address?
You visit customers and they never come to youService-area businessNo; hide it and set service areas
You visit customers and also have premises they can visit during staffed hoursHybrid businessYes, and add service areas
Customers only come to your premisesStorefront businessYes
You work entirely online with no in-person contactGenerally not eligible for a Business ProfileNot applicable

If you work from home and customers do not visit, Google's guidelines say you should not show your home address on your profile.

Checklist: Google Business Profile setup

  • Claim and verify the profile. Google offers several verification methods; which ones you see depends on your business. Service-area businesses still need a real address for verification even though it will be hidden.
  • Use your real business name. No added keywords or town names that are not part of your legal or trading name.
  • Choose the most specific primary category for your main service, and a few accurate secondary categories.
  • Hide your address if customers do not visit you.
  • Set your service areas. Add the cities, towns or postcodes you genuinely serve. Google limits how many areas you can add and advises keeping the overall area to a reasonable distance from your base. Listing the whole country does not help and is not what the feature is for.
  • Set accurate hours, including when you take calls if that differs from when you work on site.
  • Add your services with plain descriptions in customers' words.
  • Write a clear description of what you do and where. Keep it factual.
  • Add real photos: your van, your team, your equipment and examples of finished work, with the customer's permission where their property is visible.

Checklist: what not to do

  • Do not use a virtual office, mailbox service or a friend's address to appear in another town. Google's guidelines treat these as ineligible, and they are a common reason for suspension.
  • Do not create multiple profiles for the same business to cover more areas.
  • Do not stuff town names into your business name.
  • Do not buy reviews or offer incentives for them.

Competitors who do these things sometimes appear to get away with it. You can report policy violations through Google's tools. Copying them puts your own profile at risk.

Checklist: consistent details across the web

  • Decide on one official version of your business name, phone number and website.
  • For a hidden address, keep the address consistent on listings where an address is required, or leave it off where the directory allows.
  • Update Apple Business Connect and Bing Places, not only Google.
  • Claim relevant directories and trade association listings.
  • Search for old phone numbers and old trading names, and fix listings that use them.

Checklist: your website

For a service-area business, the website does much of the work of reaching beyond the map pack. Google has said that how well a business ranks in web results feeds into its local prominence.

  • A page for each main service. "Boiler repair," "bathroom installation" and "emergency call-outs" each deserve a clear page explaining what is included, how it works and how to book.
  • A clear statement of the areas you serve, on a dedicated page or in the footer.
  • Area pages only if they are genuinely useful. A page for a town you serve can work if it says something real: typical jobs there, travel times, local regulations or examples of work. Dozens of near-identical pages with only the town name swapped are thin content and can do more harm than good.
  • Your phone number visible and tappable on mobile, at the top of every page.
  • A simple contact or quote form that asks only what you need.
  • Fast loading on phones, since many service searches happen on mobile, often urgently.
  • LocalBusiness structured data, with service area information where appropriate. Test it with Google's Rich Results Test.

Checklist: reviews from every job

  • Ask every customer when the job is finished and checked, ideally the same day.
  • Send a direct link to your review form by text or email.
  • Ask for an honest review, not a five-star one.
  • Do not filter who you ask based on whether they seemed happy.
  • Reply to every review. Mentioning the type of job naturally in replies can help readers, but do not force keywords.

Checklist: earning local prominence

  • Join relevant trade associations and accreditation schemes that list members.
  • Support local events, sports clubs or charities where it fits your business.
  • Work with complementary businesses; a kitchen fitter and an electrician can genuinely refer each other.
  • Share useful local advice, such as preparing pipes for a cold snap, on your website and social channels.
  • Keep vans and uniforms branded with your real name; offline recognition is part of how well known you are.

Checklist: monthly maintenance

  1. Check your Business Profile for suggested edits from Google or the public, and correct anything wrong.
  2. Update holiday hours ahead of time.
  3. Add a few new photos of recent work.
  4. Reply to any new reviews.
  5. Check the profile's performance figures for calls and website clicks.
  6. Review your service areas; if you have stopped covering a town, remove it.

Service-area businesses often feel at a disadvantage in local search because they cannot show a shopfront. In practice, the ones that follow the rules, keep details accurate and collect reviews from every job tend to do well over time. Questions readers ask

Should I show my home address on Google?

If customers do not visit you, Google's guidelines say to hide the address and set service areas instead.

How many service areas can I add?

Google sets a limit and advises keeping the overall area within a reasonable distance of your base. Add only areas you genuinely serve.

Can I use a virtual office to appear in another town?

No. Google treats virtual offices and mailbox addresses as ineligible, and they are a common reason for profile suspension.

Do I need a separate page for every town I serve?

Only if each page says something genuinely useful. Many near-identical pages with the town name swapped are thin content.

Do service-area businesses still need to verify their profile?

Yes. Google still verifies the business, using a real address that stays hidden from the public.
