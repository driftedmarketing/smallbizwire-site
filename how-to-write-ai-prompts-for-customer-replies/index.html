How to write AI prompts for customer replies

A step-by-step method for getting drafts that sound like your business, state the right facts and need little editing.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Give the assistant four things: who you are and how you sound, the customer's message, the facts it may use such as prices and policies, and what the reply should achieve. Ask for a short draft, tell it not to invent anything missing, and always read and edit before sending.

Most disappointing AI replies come from thin prompts. "Reply to this customer" gives the assistant no idea who you are, what you can offer or what tone your customers expect, so it fills the gaps with generic politeness and, sometimes, invented facts. A better prompt takes a minute longer to write and saves several minutes of editing. Once you have a good one, you reuse it.

What goes into a good customer-reply prompt?

Every strong prompt for customer replies has the same five parts:

  1. Role and voice. Who the reply is from and how the business sounds.
  2. The customer's message. Pasted in full, clearly marked.
  3. The facts. Prices, policies, stock, dates and anything else the reply may state.
  4. The goal. What the reply should achieve: resolve, apologise, upsell, book, decline.
  5. The format. Length, sign-off, and what to do if information is missing.

Step 1: How do you describe your voice?

Write three or four sentences about how your business talks, and save them. For example: "We are a small family bakery. We write warmly but briefly, use first names, avoid exclamation marks and never use corporate phrases like 'we apologise for any inconvenience'." Specific dislikes are as useful as likes. If you have past replies you are proud of, paste one or two as examples of tone.

Step 2: How should you give it the facts?

The assistant does not know your prices, delivery times or refund policy unless you tell it. If you leave them out, it may guess. Paste the relevant facts directly into the prompt, or keep a short "facts sheet" you add each time:

  • Current prices for the products or services in question.
  • Delivery or turnaround times.
  • Refund, exchange and cancellation policy.
  • Opening hours and contact options.
  • Anything you will not do, such as discounting or rush orders.

Then add one instruction that prevents most mistakes: "Use only the facts provided. If something the customer asks is not covered, say we will check and get back to them, and list what is missing at the end."

Step 3: How do you state the goal?

Tell the assistant what a good outcome looks like. "Resolve the complaint and offer a replacement, not a refund" produces a different reply from "Apologise and offer a full refund." If the customer is a long-standing client, say so. If you want to keep the door open for future work while declining this job, say that too.

Step 4: How do you control the format?

AI replies tend to run long. Set limits: "Under 120 words. No bullet points. Sign off as Sara." For messaging apps, ask for two or three short sentences. For formal quotes, ask for a clear structure. If you want options, ask for two versions with different tones and pick one.

What does a full prompt look like?

Here is a reusable template you can adapt:

  1. "You are drafting a reply for [business name], a [what you do]. Our voice: [three sentences]."
  2. "The customer wrote: [paste message]."
  3. "Facts you may use: [prices, policy, dates]. Use only these. Do not invent anything."
  4. "Goal: [what the reply should achieve]."
  5. "Format: under [number] words, [sign-off], no [things to avoid]. If information is missing, say we will check, and list the gaps after the draft."

How do you edit the draft quickly?

Read every draft before sending, and check it in this order:

  1. Facts. Is every price, date and promise correct and something you can deliver?
  2. Customer details. Right name, right order, right issue.
  3. Tone. Would you say this out loud to this customer?
  4. Length. Cut anything the customer did not ask about.
  5. Generic phrases. Remove filler like "I hope this email finds you well" if it is not how you write.

If you make the same edit three times, add it to your voice description so the next draft gets it right.

What should you never put in a prompt?

Do not paste passwords, card numbers, bank details or government ID numbers. Check your assistant's data terms before pasting customer names and addresses, and consider removing details the draft does not need. Our article on AI assistants and inbox safety explains what to look for.

How do you build a prompt library?

Save your voice description, facts sheet and the five or six goal lines you use most in a shared document. Many assistants let you save custom instructions or reusable assistants, which saves pasting the same context each time. Update the facts sheet whenever prices or policies change; a stale facts sheet produces confidently wrong replies. Should replies be sent automatically?

No, not to customers. Even a well-prompted assistant occasionally misreads a message or states something wrong. Keep a person reading and sending every customer reply. The time saving comes from not writing from scratch, not from skipping the check.

The bottom line

Good customer-reply prompts are mostly context: your voice, your facts, your goal and your format. Write them once, reuse them, refine them when you catch yourself making the same edit, and keep your own eyes on every reply before it goes out.

Questions readers ask

Why do AI replies sound so generic?

Usually because the prompt lacks a voice description and examples. Describe how your business writes, including phrases you dislike, and paste a past reply you liked.

How do I stop the AI inventing prices or policies?

Paste the facts into the prompt and tell it to use only those, to say you will check if something is missing, and to list any gaps after the draft.

How long should a customer reply be?

Usually shorter than the AI's default. Set a word limit, such as under 120 words for email or two or three sentences for messaging apps.

Can I reuse the same prompt every time?

Yes. Keep a saved voice description, a facts sheet and a few goal lines, and swap in the customer's message each time.

Should I let AI send customer replies automatically?

No. Have a person read and send every customer reply, since even good drafts occasionally contain errors.
