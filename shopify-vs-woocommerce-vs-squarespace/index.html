Shopify vs WooCommerce vs Squarespace: which is best for a small shop?

A fair comparison of the three platforms small shops most often choose between, and the questions that decide it.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Shopify suits most shops that want to sell seriously without managing technology, because hosting, checkout and security are handled for you. WooCommerce suits owners who want full control and are comfortable maintaining a WordPress site. Squarespace suits businesses whose shop is a smaller part of a design-led website. Check each platform's current pricing before deciding.

Choosing an ecommerce platform is one of the few decisions in a small business that is genuinely painful to reverse. Moving products, customers, order history and search rankings from one platform to another is possible, but it takes time and carries risk. So it is worth an hour of honest thinking before you start.

Shopify, WooCommerce and Squarespace are three of the most common choices for small shops. They are built on very different ideas, and each is the right answer for some businesses and the wrong answer for others. This comparison sticks to well-known, stable differences. Prices, fees and plan names change often, so check each platform's own pricing page on the day you decide rather than trusting any figure in an article, including this one.

What is the basic difference between the three?

  • Shopify is a hosted platform built specifically for selling. You pay a monthly subscription and Shopify runs the servers, security, checkout and updates.
  • WooCommerce is a free, open-source plugin that turns a WordPress website into a shop. The software is free, but you arrange and pay for hosting, and you are responsible for updates, security and backups, or for paying someone to handle them.
  • Squarespace is a hosted website builder known for polished templates, with commerce features built in. It is a website-first tool that also sells, rather than a shop-first tool.

What does each platform really cost?

The headline monthly price rarely tells the whole story. When comparing, add up:

  1. The base plan. Shopify and Squarespace publish tiered plans. WooCommerce itself is free.
  2. Hosting. Included with Shopify and Squarespace. For WooCommerce, quality managed WordPress hosting is a real monthly cost, and cheap shared hosting can make a busy shop slow.
  3. Payment processing. All three charge or pass on card processing fees. Shopify has historically charged an extra transaction fee when you use a third-party payment provider instead of its own; check whether that applies to you and in your country.
  4. Themes. Free themes exist on all three. Premium themes are a one-off or annual cost.
  5. Apps, plugins and extensions. This is where costs creep. Many useful apps charge monthly.
  6. Your time or a developer's time. WooCommerce in particular needs ongoing maintenance: plugin updates, compatibility checks and backups.

A fair comparison is a year's total cost of the setup you actually need, not the cheapest plan on each pricing page.

When is Shopify the right choice?

Shopify tends to suit businesses where the shop is the business. Its strengths are a fast, trusted checkout, built-in inventory and order management, a large app store, and the fact that you never have to think about servers or security patches. It handles growth well: a store with ten products and a store with thousands run on the same system.

The trade-offs are that you work within Shopify's structure. Some URL patterns are fixed, deep checkout customisation is limited on lower plans, and monthly app costs can add up. If you go this way, our Shopify SEO guide covers how to work with its structure rather than against it.

When is WooCommerce the right choice?

WooCommerce suits owners who want full ownership. Because it runs on WordPress, you can change almost anything, move to any host, and use WordPress's very strong publishing tools for a content-heavy site. If your business already runs on WordPress, adding WooCommerce can be the simplest path.

The trade-off is responsibility. You, or someone you pay, must keep WordPress, WooCommerce, the theme and every plugin updated and compatible. Security is your job. Performance depends on your hosting. For a business without technical help, that responsibility is the real cost.

When is Squarespace the right choice?

Squarespace suits businesses whose website is mostly about presentation, with selling as one part: a photographer selling prints, a studio selling a few classes, a restaurant selling gift cards, a maker with a small catalogue. Its templates are polished and its editor is easy for non-technical owners.

The trade-off is depth. For larger catalogues, complex variants, wholesale pricing or heavy integrations, owners often find they outgrow it. That is not a flaw so much as a matter of focus.

What questions should decide it for my shop?

  • How many products will you sell in two years? A handful favours simplicity; hundreds favour a commerce-first platform.
  • Who will maintain it? If the answer is "nobody technical," lean toward a hosted platform.
  • How important is content? If blogging and long articles are central, WordPress's publishing tools are a real advantage.
  • Where do you sell besides your site? Check which platforms connect cleanly with the marketplaces and social channels you use.
  • What payment providers work in your country? Availability varies by region. Check before you commit.
  • What is your total yearly budget? Including apps and help, not just the base plan.

How hard is it to switch later?

All three let you export products and customers, and migration tools and services exist. The hard parts are usually redirects from old URLs to new ones (so you keep your search rankings), rebuilding design, reconnecting apps and re-testing checkout. Planning redirects properly is the single most important step. Whatever you choose, build a clear page for each product from the start; our product page guide covers principles that apply on any platform.

Most platforms offer a free trial. Use it seriously: add five real products, run a test order, and try editing a page on your phone. An afternoon of hands-on testing will tell you more than any comparison table.

Questions readers ask

Is WooCommerce really free?

The plugin is free, but you pay for hosting, and often for a premium theme, extensions and maintenance. Compare a full year's cost, not the software price alone.

Which platform is easiest for a beginner?

Shopify and Squarespace are generally easier because hosting and security are handled for you. WooCommerce is easier if you already know WordPress.

Does Shopify charge transaction fees?

Shopify has historically added a fee when you use a third-party payment provider instead of its own. Check its current pricing page for your plan and country.

Can I move from Squarespace to Shopify later?

Yes. Products and customers can be exported. Plan redirects from old URLs to new ones carefully so you keep your search rankings.

Which is best for SEO?

All three can rank well. Content quality, structure and speed matter far more than the platform choice for most small shops.
