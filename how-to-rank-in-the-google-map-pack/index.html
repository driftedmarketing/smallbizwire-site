How do I rank in the Google map pack?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Here is what you can influence in each.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Google says local results depend mainly on three factors: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the searcher or the searched location) and prominence (how well known your business is). You cannot change distance, so focus on a complete, accurate Business Profile, genuine reviews, a strong website and real local reputation.

The map pack is the block of local businesses, usually three, that appears with a map near the top of Google results when someone searches for something like "plumber near me" or "coffee in Clifton." For many local businesses, it is the most valuable spot on the internet. It is also the subject of a great deal of confident guesswork.

The useful starting point is what Google itself says. In its Business Profile help documentation, Google states that local results are based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. It also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Everything else in this article builds on those three factors.

What are the three factors Google names?

FactorWhat Google meansCan you influence it?
RelevanceHow well a local profile matches what someone is searching forYes, through a complete and accurate profile
DistanceHow far each result is from the location used in the searchNot really; your location is your location
ProminenceHow well known a business is, including information from across the web, reviews and ratingsYes, over time

Google has also said that a business's position in general web results is a factor in prominence. In other words, your website's overall search performance can help your map visibility too.

How do I improve relevance?

Relevance is about giving Google a clear, complete picture of what you do. Most of it lives in your Google Business Profile.

  • Choose the right primary category. This is one of the most important choices on the profile. Pick the most specific category that describes your core business, then add a few accurate secondary categories. Do not add categories for services you do not really offer.
  • Fill in every relevant field. Hours, services or products, attributes, service areas, description and opening date. Google says complete, accurate information helps it match you to searches.
  • Use your real business name. Adding keywords to the name breaks Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended.
  • List your services clearly. Where the profile allows services or products, add them with plain descriptions that match how customers speak.
  • Keep hours accurate, including holiday hours. A business shown as closed is unlikely to be shown for "open now" searches.
  • Make your website match. Your site should have clear pages for your main services and your location, with your name, address and phone number consistent with your profile.

What can I do about distance?

Very little, and that is worth accepting. If someone searches "dentist" while standing across town, a dentist next to them has an advantage. If they search "dentist in [your neighbourhood]," Google uses that location instead.

What you should not do is create fake addresses, virtual offices or mailbox addresses to appear in more places. Google's guidelines prohibit listing a location where you do not have a real, staffed presence that meets its eligibility rules, and profiles that break this can be removed. Service-area businesses, such as tradespeople who travel to customers, have a legitimate way to show the areas they cover; see our local SEO checklist for service-area businesses.

The honest approach to distance is to compete hardest where you actually are, and to use your website and other marketing to reach further.

How do I build prominence?

Prominence is the slowest factor to build and the hardest for a competitor to copy. It reflects how well known and well regarded your business is, online and off.

  • Reviews. Google explicitly mentions review count and rating. Ask every customer, make it easy, and reply to every review. Our guide on getting more Google reviews without breaking the rules covers the method.
  • Consistent listings. Accurate name, address and phone details across the web help Google connect information to your business. See what NAP consistency is.
  • Links and mentions. Local press coverage, sponsorships, partnerships, trade associations and community organisations that mention or link to you.
  • A strong website. Since Google says web ranking feeds into prominence, a useful, fast, well-structured site helps.
  • Real-world reputation. Google describes prominence as reflecting how well known a business is offline too. Famous local landmarks and well-known brands tend to be more prominent. You cannot fake this, but you can earn it.

Do photos and posts help?

Google encourages businesses to add photos and keep information up to date, and it says that adding photos helps customers. Whether posts or photos directly change rankings is not something Google states, and claims that they do are mostly speculation. They do, however, help the people looking at your profile decide to call you, which is the point of ranking in the first place. Add real photos of your premises, team and work, and keep them current.

What gets businesses penalised?

  • Keyword-stuffed business names.
  • Fake or ineligible addresses.
  • Duplicate profiles for the same location.
  • Fake reviews, paid reviews or incentivised reviews.
  • Categories that do not match the real business.

Some competitors get away with these tactics for a while, which is frustrating. Google provides a way to suggest edits to listings and report policy violations. Use those tools rather than copying the behaviour.

How long does it take to rank in the map pack?

There is no reliable timeline. Relevance improvements, such as fixing a wrong category, can show effects relatively quickly. Prominence builds over months and years. Competition matters too: a new café in a street full of established cafés faces a different challenge from the only electrician in a small town.

What is a sensible plan?

  1. Claim and verify your Business Profile.
  2. Choose the most accurate primary category and complete every relevant field.
  3. Make sure your name, address and phone match everywhere.
  4. Set up a routine to ask every customer for a review.
  5. Build website pages for your main services and location.
  6. Earn local mentions through genuine community and industry involvement.
  7. Review your profile monthly and keep it accurate.

None of this is secret. The businesses that win the map pack over time are usually the ones that do these unglamorous things consistently, while competitors chase shortcuts.

Questions readers ask

What are Google's three local ranking factors?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence.

Can I pay Google to rank higher in the map pack?

No. Google says you cannot pay for a better organic local ranking. Local ads are separate and labelled as ads.

Does adding keywords to my business name help?

It breaks Google's Business Profile guidelines and can lead to suspension. Use your real business name.

Can I use a virtual office to rank in another area?

Google's guidelines do not allow ineligible addresses such as virtual offices without a real staffed presence. Profiles that break this can be removed.

Do reviews affect map pack rankings?

Google says review count and score factor into local ranking as part of prominence.

How long does it take to rank?

There is no fixed timeline. Fixing relevance issues can help quickly, while prominence builds over months and depends on your competition.
