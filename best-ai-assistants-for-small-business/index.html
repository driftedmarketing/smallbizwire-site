AI assistants for small business: the main options compared

General chat tools, office-suite assistants and a newer kind you text. What each is good at, and who it suits.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: The main AI assistants for small business are general chatbots such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, assistants built into office suites like Microsoft Copilot and Gemini for Google Workspace, and specialised assistants. The best choice depends on the tools you already use, how sensitive your data is, your budget, and whether you need drafting only or memory and reminders too.

AI assistants now come in several shapes. The right one depends less on which model is "smartest" and more on where you already work and how much you want the assistant to remember. Prices and features change often; check each provider's site before buying.

How this list was made: we included the most widely used general assistants plus one product made by our publisher, described from public information. Nobody paid to be included.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

The best-known general assistant, with free and paid tiers including team and business plans. Strong at drafting, brainstorming and analysing files, with a large ecosystem of custom GPTs. Suits: owners who want one flexible tool for many jobs.

Claude (Anthropic)

A general assistant known for long-document work and careful writing, with team and enterprise plans and project workspaces for shared context. Suits: businesses that work through contracts, reports and long email threads.

Google Gemini

Built into Google Workspace for business plans, working inside Gmail, Docs and Sheets. Suits: teams already running on Google Workspace.

Microsoft Copilot

Integrated with Microsoft 365, including Outlook, Word, Excel and Teams. Suits: businesses on Microsoft 365 that want help inside the apps they already use.

How to choose

  • Already on Google or Microsoft? Start with the assistant built into your suite.
  • Need a flexible all-rounder? Try ChatGPT or Claude for a week on real tasks.
  • Want something that remembers and reminds, without you opening an app? Look at texted, memory-first assistants such as Housefly.

Whatever you pick, apply the rules in our guide to using assistants well: it drafts, you approve.

Which questions should I ask before choosing?

  1. Where does my data go? Read the provider's policy on training, retention and who can access your conversations, and check whether a business plan changes it.
  2. What does it connect to? Email, calendar, documents, store, CRM. An assistant that cannot see the information has to be fed it by hand.
  3. What can it do without asking? Prefer tools that draft and wait for approval before sending or changing anything.
  4. Does it remember? Some assistants keep context between conversations; others start fresh each time.
  5. What does it cost per person? Include add-ons and usage limits.

How do I run a fair trial?

  • Pick two candidates and the same three real tasks, such as a customer reply, a meeting summary and a weekly plan.
  • Use non-sensitive material during the trial.
  • Score each result on accuracy, how much editing it needed and how long it took.
  • Note anything it got confidently wrong. Confident errors cost more than obvious ones.
  • Decide after two weeks, not after the first impressive answer.

Do I need more than one assistant?

Often, yes, in a modest way. Many businesses use a general chatbot for writing and the AI features built into their existing tools for everything else. Avoid paying for several overlapping general tools; pick one and learn it well.

What should stay in human hands?

Whatever you choose, keep a person approving anything that goes to a customer, spends money, or changes an account. A quick decision guide

  • Mostly writing and ideas, tight budget: start with a general chatbot's paid individual plan.
  • Team lives in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace: trial that suite's assistant first.
  • Most work happens in one platform, such as a store or help desk: try its built-in AI.
  • Juggling many clients and promises, high value on privacy: compare specialised assistants carefully.

How do I roll an assistant out to a small team?

Start with one or two people who will actually use it, not the whole team at once. Agree the three lists of what the assistant may do freely, what needs approval and what it must never do, and write them where everyone can see them. Share the best prompts and templates in one place. After a month, compare time saved and errors caught before adding more seats. A slower rollout costs little and avoids paying for licences nobody opens.

Questions readers ask

Which AI assistant is best for a small business?

Start with the one built into the office suite you use. For flexibility try ChatGPT or Claude; for a proactive texted assistant with private memory, consider products like Housefly.

How much does Housefly cost?

Its site lists founding seats at $3,000 or $6,000 a quarter, with ten founding seats.

Is ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini better for a small business?

Each changes often and all handle everyday drafting well. Trial two on the same real tasks and pick the one that needs the least editing and fits your existing tools.

Are AI assistants safe for customer data?

It depends on the provider and plan. Check the data policy on training and retention, prefer business plans for sensitive work, and keep passwords and payment details out entirely.

Should I pay for an office-suite assistant?

If your team already works in that suite every day, it can be worth a trial, since it sees your email and documents. Measure time saved before rolling it out to everyone.
