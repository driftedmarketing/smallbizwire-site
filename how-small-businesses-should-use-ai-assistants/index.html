How small businesses should actually use AI assistants

Skip the novelty. The useful jobs are drafting, remembering and reminding, with a human saying yes before anything goes out.

SmallBizWire Editors · 30 October 2026 · 2 min read

Disclosure: Housefly is made by Drifted Marketing, which publishes SmallBizWire.

Most small businesses meet AI assistants the same way: someone asks a chatbot to write a caption, the result is fine but bland, and the tool gets filed under "interesting." That is a waste. The owners getting real value use assistants for a narrower, duller set of jobs, and they set firm rules about what the assistant may do on its own.

Start with the jobs that eat your week

The best first uses are the ones you already do by hand, repeatedly, and resent:

  • First drafts. Replies to common customer questions, follow-up emails after a call, a quote cover note, a product description. You edit; the blank page is gone.
  • Summaries. A long email thread, a supplier contract, a meeting transcript, boiled down to what was promised and by whom.
  • Memory. What you told a client last month, which supplier is late, which invoice is unpaid. An assistant that keeps this is worth more than one that writes beautifully.
  • Prompts to act. A reminder that arrives before the deadline, not a dashboard you have to remember to open.

Keep a human on every send

The rule that separates a helpful assistant from a liability is simple: it drafts, you send. Anything that emails a customer, spends money, changes a live account or publishes in your name should stop and ask first, and the question should state plainly what will happen. "Send the revised quote to this client from your email?" is a good question. "Proceed?" is not.

This matters because AI models are confident even when wrong. A wrong draft costs you thirty seconds of editing. A wrong email sent automatically costs you a client.

Be careful what you feed it

Before pasting customer data into any tool, check three things: whether your inputs are used to train the provider's models, how long conversations are stored, and whether other people in a shared workspace can see them. Business tiers of the major assistants generally offer stronger data terms than free consumer plans. Read them; they differ.

Never paste passwords, card numbers or government ID numbers into a chat window. No legitimate workflow needs them there.

Choose the shape that fits how you work

General chat assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot are flexible and inexpensive, and they suit owners who will sit down and use them deliberately. Some newer products take a different shape: an assistant you text, which keeps a running memory of your business and speaks up when something needs you. A one-week trial plan

  1. Pick three recurring tasks from the list above.
  2. Use an assistant for each, every time it comes up, for five working days.
  3. Note how long each took before and after, and how much editing the drafts needed.
  4. Keep what saved time. Drop what did not.

That small test tells you more than any demo.

Questions readers ask

Should an AI assistant send emails for my business automatically?

Generally no. Let it draft, and keep a person approving anything that goes to a customer, spends money or changes an account.

Is it safe to put customer information into an AI chatbot?

It depends on the provider's data terms. Check whether inputs are used for training, how long they are retained and who can see them, and never paste passwords or payment details.

What is the best first task to give an AI assistant?

A repetitive writing job you already do weekly, such as follow-up emails or answers to common customer questions.
