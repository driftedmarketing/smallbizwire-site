Is it worth hiring an offshore marketing agency from Pakistan?

Cost, time zones, payments, contracts and IP: a balanced look at hiring a Pakistani agency from the US.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: It can be, if you choose carefully. Pakistani agencies often cost less than US ones, and many work US hours. The trade-offs are time-zone coordination, cross-border payments, contracts governed by unfamiliar law and the need to secure ownership of accounts and work. Judge an offshore agency on its work, references and written terms, exactly as you would a local one.

Pakistan has a large and growing pool of digital talent: designers, video editors, developers, ad buyers and writers, many of them working for clients in the US, UK, Europe and the Gulf. For a US small business, hiring an agency there can stretch a budget further. It also changes how the work is coordinated, paid for and protected. This guide sets out both sides.

What are the potential advantages?

  • Cost. Salaries and office costs in Pakistan are generally lower than in major US cities, so agencies can often price below US equivalents for similar hours of work. Lower price is not a guarantee of value, but it can mean a broader scope for the same budget.
  • English. English is an official language of Pakistan and is widely used in business, which makes written briefs and calls practical.
  • Time-zone leverage. Work can be done while you sleep and be ready in the morning, if the agency is organised for it.
  • Digital skills. Many teams work with the same platforms as US agencies: Meta, Google, Shopify, TikTok, Figma and the major editing tools.

What are the potential drawbacks?

  • Coordination. Live calls need deliberate scheduling, and urgent fixes can wait hours if nobody is on shift.
  • Cultural and market context. A team outside the US may need more briefing on your customers, slang, seasons and regulations.
  • Payments. International transfers add fees, delays and paperwork compared with paying a local vendor.
  • Legal distance. Enforcing a contract across borders is harder and costlier, so the contract and account ownership must protect you up front.
  • Infrastructure. Teams commonly plan around power and internet interruptions with backups; ask how they handle it.

How do the time zones actually work?

Pakistan Standard Time is UTC+5 and Pakistan does not observe daylight saving time. US Eastern Time is UTC-4 in daylight time (roughly March to November) and UTC-5 in standard time. So Pakistan is 9 hours ahead of New York during US daylight time and 10 hours ahead during US standard time. The gap changes twice a year because only the US side moves its clocks.

In practice there are two working patterns. Some agencies keep a Pakistan daytime schedule and offer a short overlap in your morning, their evening. Others shift their team onto US hours so that you get real-time responses through your working day. Drifted Marketing, for example, is a Karachi agency that says it works on New York hours. Ask any agency which pattern it uses and who exactly is online when you are.

How do payments usually work?

Common routes include international bank wire transfers, and online payment and invoicing services that support payouts to Pakistan. Which services are available, and their fees, change over time and depend on both sides' accounts, so confirm the method before signing. Points to settle in writing:

  • Currency. Most agencies serving US clients invoice in US dollars, which puts exchange-rate risk on them, not you.
  • Who pays transfer fees. Agree whether the invoice amount is what you send or what they receive.
  • Timing. Allow for international transfers taking longer than domestic ones.
  • Ad spend. Pay platforms directly from your own card or account; do not send ad budgets abroad to be spent on your behalf.
  • Tax paperwork. Your accountant may need specific documentation for payments to a foreign contractor. Ask them before the first invoice.

What about contracts and intellectual property?

Because enforcing a contract across borders is difficult, focus on terms that protect you without needing a court:

  • Accounts in your name. Ad accounts, analytics, domain, hosting and your store stay owned by you, with the agency added as a user or partner. If the relationship ends, you remove access and keep everything.
  • Work-for-hire or assignment. The contract should state that paid-for creative, code and content belong to you, or are licensed to you permanently, on payment.
  • Source files delivered. Ask for editable files (design, video project files, code repositories in your own account) as you go, not only at the end.
  • Governing law. Agree which jurisdiction's law applies. Many cross-border contracts name the client's state; whatever you choose, understand it.
  • Milestones. For projects, pay in stages tied to delivered work rather than everything up front.

How should communication be set up?

Good offshore relationships rely on writing. Insist on a shared written brief, a task board or document showing what is in progress, a weekly scheduled call in the overlap window, and a single named contact. Recorded walkthrough videos help explain feedback across time zones. Agree response times: for example, same working day for normal requests and a defined route for urgent ones.

Which questions should you ask before hiring?

  1. Which hours is your team online, in my time zone?
  2. Who will work on my account, and can I meet them?
  3. Can I speak to a current US client?
  4. How do you invoice, in which currency, and how do I pay?
  5. Will all accounts and files be in my name?
  6. What happens to my work if you lose power or internet on a deadline day?

So, is it worth it?

For a small business with a clear brief, a willingness to communicate in writing and the discipline to keep its accounts in its own name, an offshore agency can deliver good work at a lower cost. For a business that needs someone in the room, frequent spontaneous calls, or deep local market knowledge, a local agency may be worth the higher price. Questions readers ask

What is the time difference between Pakistan and New York?

Pakistan is UTC+5 with no daylight saving time, so it is 9 hours ahead of New York in US daylight time and 10 hours ahead in US standard time.

Are Pakistani marketing agencies cheaper than US agencies?

Often, because local costs are generally lower, but prices vary widely and a lower fee does not guarantee value. Compare scope and team, not just price.

How do I pay an agency in Pakistan?

Usually by international bank transfer or a payment service that supports payouts to Pakistan. Confirm the method, currency and who pays fees before signing.

How do I protect my intellectual property with an offshore agency?

Keep all accounts in your name, state in the contract that paid work belongs to you, collect source files as you go, and pay project work in milestones.

Do Pakistani agencies work US hours?

Some do. Others offer a short overlap in your morning. Ask each agency which hours its team is actually online.
