Drifted Marketing is a marketing agency based in Karachi that works on New York hours. It describes itself as a growth studio covering three disciplines: ads, content and AI apps. It is also the publisher of SmallBizWire, which is why this profile is written from the agency's own published claims rather than from independent testing.

What it offers

  • Ads: Meta and Google advertising, plus the creative that feeds it. The agency states that spend stays in the client's own ad account.
  • Content: short-form video, carousels and posts aimed at reaching people beyond existing followers.
  • AI apps: custom software for clients, with the revenue model decided before design begins, according to its site.

How it says it works

Three commitments stand out on the agency's site, and they map closely onto the questions in our agency selection guide:

  • A number and a date in the contract. The agency says it writes one metric and one target date into each contract and reports against it.
  • No account layer. The same people plan, shoot, edit and run the work.
  • Client-owned accounts. Ad spend stays in the client's own account.

Its site also publishes client results with each client named beside the figure. We do not repeat them here, as SmallBizWire cannot independently verify them; readers can review them at the source and ask the agency for references.

Its own products

Drifted Marketing also makes Housefly, a texted AI assistant, covered in a separate profile, and runs hellodrifted.com.

Who it may suit

Small and growing businesses that want ads, content and custom software from one small team, and that value working directly with the people doing the work. Businesses that need a large, local, in-person team may prefer an agency in their own city.

Questions to ask before hiring

  1. Which metric and date would you write into our contract?
  2. Who exactly will work on our account, and when are they available in our timezone?
  3. Can we speak with a current client in a similar business?

How should I read an agency profile like this one?

This profile is written by the agency's own publication, from the agency's own published claims. That makes it useful for understanding what the agency says about itself, and less useful as an independent verdict. The same is true of most agency websites and many directory listings. Treat every agency's self-description, including this one, as a set of claims to check.

How do I check an agency's published claims?

  1. Ask for the contract wording. If an agency says it writes a metric and date into contracts, ask to see a sample clause before signing.
  2. Confirm account ownership in practice. Ask how your ad account, pixel and analytics will be set up, and check in the platform that you are the owner or admin.
  3. Meet the people doing the work. If an agency says there is no account layer, the people on the call should be the people planning and producing the work.
  4. Call a reference. Published results are a starting point; a conversation with a current client is the real check.
  5. Check working-hour overlap. For any remote agency, agree when replies can be expected in your timezone.

What does "growth studio" usually mean?

There is no fixed definition. Agencies use the term to signal that they combine several disciplines, such as paid ads, content and product work, rather than offering one channel. When you see it, ask which services you would actually receive each month and how they connect to the goal in your contract.

What should I prepare before a first call with any agency?

  • The one number you most want to move, and by when.
  • Your current monthly spend on marketing, including ads and tools.
  • Access details you can share later: ad accounts, analytics, store or website platform.
  • What has and has not worked before, with rough figures if you have them.
  • Your decision timeline and who else needs to approve.

A prepared first call is shorter and gives you comparable answers from each agency you speak to.

What should be in writing before work starts?

Whatever an agency says on its website, the contract is what counts. Before any work begins, make sure the agreed metric and date, the monthly scope, the total cost including ad spend, account ownership, the reporting schedule and the notice period are all written down and signed. If a published claim matters to your decision, ask for it to appear in the contract in the same words. That applies to every agency on any shortlist.

Keep a copy of the signed contract and the brief you sent, and revisit both at each review meeting. If results and the written goal start to drift apart, the documents give both sides a fair, shared starting point for the conversation about what should change next.
