Google Business Profile: the local SEO job most small businesses leave half-done

For anyone serving a local area, this free listing often matters more than the website.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: To get the most from Google Business Profile, verify your listing, choose the most accurate primary category, keep your name, address and phone consistent everywhere, set correct hours including holidays, add real photos, list services or products, ask every happy customer for a review and reply to all reviews, and post updates regularly. The profile itself is free.

When someone searches "plumber near me" or "cafe open now", the map results usually appear before any website. Those results come from Google Business Profile. It is free, and most profiles are incomplete.

Get the basics exactly right

  • Name: your real business name. Adding keywords to the name breaches Google's guidelines and can get a listing suspended.
  • Category: the primary category matters a great deal. Choose the most specific accurate one, then add secondary categories.
  • Address or service area: show an address only if customers visit you there.
  • Hours, including holiday hours. Wrong hours generate bad reviews quickly.
  • Phone and website, matching what is on your site.

Fill in what competitors skip

  • Services or products with short descriptions and prices where sensible.
  • Real photos: the outside (so people can find you), the inside, the team, the work.
  • Attributes such as accessibility and payment options.
  • The business description, written for people rather than keywords.

Reviews

Ask every satisfied customer, with a direct link to your review form. Reply to every review, good or bad, briefly and professionally. Do not offer incentives for reviews and never post fake ones; both break Google's policies, and fake reviews are increasingly targeted by consumer-protection regulators.

Keep it alive

Post updates, offers or events occasionally, answer the Questions section, and update photos seasonally. An active profile signals a business that is open and cared for.

Connect it to your website

Make sure your name, address and phone number appear identically on your website, and add LocalBusiness structured data. Consistency across the web helps Google trust the details. For broader visibility, including in AI assistants' answers, see our guide to AI search.

How do I get more Google reviews without breaking the rules?

  • Ask every customer at the moment they are happiest, such as when a job is finished.
  • Use the review link from your profile dashboard and send it by text or email.
  • Make it easy: one link, one short sentence.
  • Do not offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, and do not ask only happy customers through a filtering system. Google's policies prohibit incentivised and selectively solicited reviews.
  • Never write reviews yourself or ask staff to. Fake reviews break Google's rules and, in the US, can attract action from the Federal Trade Commission.

How should I reply to reviews?

Reply to every review, good or bad. For positive ones, thank the person and mention the specific service in natural words. For negative ones, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, avoid sharing private details, and offer to resolve it offline. Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews.

Why do name, address and phone details need to match everywhere?

Search engines compare your details across your website, your profile and other directories. Inconsistent addresses or old phone numbers create doubt about which is correct. Make a list of every directory where you appear, then update each to match your profile exactly. Put the same details in your website footer and on your contact page.

Do Google Business Profile posts help?

Posts let you share offers, events and updates directly on your listing. Their effect on rankings is debated, but they give searchers recent, relevant information and show the business is active. A short post each week or two, with a real photo, is a reasonable routine.

A monthly local SEO routine

  1. Check hours, phone and website link are correct.
  2. Reply to any new reviews and questions.
  3. Add a few new photos of recent work.
  4. Publish one or two posts.
  5. Review the profile's performance insights for calls, direction requests and website clicks.
  6. Check for suggested edits from the public and accept or reject them.

How does my website support my profile?

Your profile links to your site, and Google uses both. Give each main service its own page that names the area you serve, and make the contact details obvious. What are the most common mistakes?

  • Adding keywords or city names to the business name.
  • Choosing a broad category when a more specific one exists.
  • Forgetting holiday hours, so customers arrive to a locked door.
  • Leaving reviews unanswered for weeks.
  • Using stock photos instead of real images of the business.
  • Creating a second listing instead of fixing the first.
  • Letting a former employee or old agency keep sole ownership of the profile.

Check who owns and manages your profile today, and make sure the business, not an individual or supplier, holds the primary owner role.

If you work with an agency or freelancer on local SEO, add them as a manager rather than handing over ownership. That way you can remove their access later without losing the reviews, photos and history the profile has built up over the years.

Questions readers ask

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile costs nothing.

Can I add keywords to my business name on Google?

No. Google's guidelines require your real business name, and keyword stuffing can lead to suspension.

How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

It varies by business and method, from immediate to a few weeks. Google may ask for a video, phone, email or postcard check.

Can a service-area business use Google Business Profile?

Yes. Businesses that visit customers can set a service area and hide their street address.

How do I remove a fake or unfair Google review?

You can flag a review that breaks Google's policies from your profile. Google decides whether to remove it; reply calmly in the meantime.
