ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini vs Copilot: which suits a small-business owner?

A fair comparison of the four best-known AI assistants, judged on the jobs small businesses actually give them.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: All four can draft, summarise and answer questions well. The practical difference is fit: Gemini sits inside Google Workspace, Copilot inside Microsoft 365, ChatGPT has the broadest general ecosystem, and Claude is often chosen for long documents and careful writing. Pick the one closest to the software you already use, then test it on your own tasks.

Small-business owners ask which AI assistant is "best" as if there were one answer. There is not, and the leaderboard changes every few months as each company releases new models. A more useful question is which assistant fits the way your business already works. This comparison sticks to differences that tend to hold up over time, and it avoids quoting prices that will be out of date by the time you read it.

Who makes each assistant?

  • ChatGPT is made by OpenAI. It popularised the chat assistant and has a large ecosystem of add-ons, custom assistants and integrations.
  • Claude is made by Anthropic. It is widely used for writing, analysing long documents and coding.
  • Gemini is made by Google. It is built into Google's products, including Gmail, Docs and Sheets, through Google Workspace.
  • Copilot is Microsoft's brand for its assistants, including Copilot inside Microsoft 365 apps such as Outlook, Word and Excel.

Which is best for writing customer emails?

All four write competent emails. The differences are in tone and in how much editing you will do. Some owners find one assistant's default style too enthusiastic and another's too formal. The honest answer is to give each the same three real customer emails and see which drafts you would send with the fewest changes. Our guide to writing AI prompts for customer replies shows how to brief any of them so the style gap narrows.

Which is best if you live in Gmail or Outlook?

This is where the choice becomes clearer. If your business runs on Google Workspace, Gemini's presence inside Gmail, Docs and Sheets means less copying and pasting. If you run on Microsoft 365, Copilot sits inside Outlook, Word, Excel and Teams. Convenience matters: the assistant you can reach in one click is the one you will actually use. Check which features are included in your existing plan and which need an add-on.

Which is best for long documents?

Contracts, tender documents, supplier terms and long reports are common small-business reading. All four can now handle long inputs, and limits vary by plan and model. Claude has a reputation for handling long documents and following detailed instructions closely, but the others have improved quickly. For more on fitting long-document work into your week, see how small businesses should use AI assistants. Test with a real document you already understand, and ask questions whose answers you know.

Which is best for spreadsheets and numbers?

Copilot in Excel and Gemini in Sheets have the advantage of working directly in the file. ChatGPT and Claude can analyse uploaded spreadsheets and explain formulas. For anything that ends up on an invoice or a tax return, check the arithmetic yourself or in your accounting software.

What about privacy and your data?

This is the most important comparison and the one most often skipped. Consumer and business plans usually come with different data terms. Before you put customer information into any assistant, check:

  1. Whether your inputs are used to train the provider's models, and whether you can turn that off.
  2. How long conversations and uploaded files are retained.
  3. Whether team plans give an administrator control over who can use what.
  4. Where the data is processed, if your industry or country has rules about that.

Read the current terms on each provider's site rather than relying on a summary. They change, and business plans generally offer stronger commitments than free ones. Our article on whether it is safe to give an AI assistant access to your inbox goes deeper.

How should you actually decide?

  1. Start with your software. If you are on Google Workspace, try Gemini first. On Microsoft 365, try Copilot first.
  2. Run a side-by-side test. Give two assistants the same five real tasks: a customer reply, a summary of a long thread, a product description, a polite refusal, and a question about one of your documents.
  3. Score the editing. Count how many changes you made before each result was usable.
  4. Check the data terms of the plan you would actually buy.
  5. Pick one and standardise. A team using four different assistants on personal accounts is harder to manage than a team using one well.

Do you need more than one?

Some owners keep one assistant inside their main software and a second general one for writing or research. That is reasonable. Paying for four is rarely necessary. The productivity gain comes from building habits and good prompts, not from switching tools whenever a new model is announced.

What about specialised assistants?

Beyond these four general tools, many products are built for one job: customer support inboxes, bookkeeping, ecommerce, or a connected assistant that remembers your clients. These often use one of the big models underneath. They can be worth it when they save setup work, but the same rules apply: check the data terms and keep a person approving anything sent. The AI assistants buyer's guide covers some of the options.

The bottom line

For most small businesses, the right assistant is the one already closest to where the work happens. The models are close enough in quality that fit, data terms and habit matter more than benchmark scores. Test on your own tasks, choose one, and get good at it.

Questions readers ask

Which AI assistant is best for a small business?

There is no single best. Businesses on Google Workspace often start with Gemini, Microsoft 365 users with Copilot, and ChatGPT and Claude are strong general choices. Test two on your own tasks.

Are the free versions good enough?

For occasional drafting, often yes. Paid and business plans usually add higher limits, stronger models, team controls and better data terms. Check current details on each provider's site.

Do these assistants use my data for training?

It depends on the provider and plan. Business plans generally offer stronger commitments. Read the current data terms for the exact plan you use.

Should my team all use the same assistant?

Usually yes. One business account makes data controls, shared prompts and training simpler than several personal accounts.

How often do these tools change?

Frequently. Features, models and pricing are updated several times a year, so recheck before making a long commitment.
