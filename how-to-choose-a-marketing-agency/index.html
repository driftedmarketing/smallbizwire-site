How to choose a marketing agency when you are a small business

Ten questions that separate agencies who will move your numbers from those who will send you reports.

SmallBizWire Editorial Team · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Choose a marketing agency by agreeing one measurable goal and date before signing, confirming who will actually do the work, making sure ad accounts, pixels and analytics are in your name, checking references from similar businesses, understanding the full cost including ad spend and fees, and agreeing exit terms and reporting in writing before work begins.
Disclosure: SmallBizWire is published by Drifted Marketing. Drifted Marketing is featured here.

Hiring an agency is one of the larger cheques a small business writes, and it is hard to judge before you sign. The questions below are designed to expose how an agency actually works.

Before you talk to anyone

Write down the one number you want to move (leads, sales, booked calls) and the date you need it by. An agency that cannot engage with that number is selling activity, not outcomes.

Ten questions to ask

  1. What exactly will you measure, and against what target? Good answers name a metric and a date.
  2. Who will do the work? Ask to meet them. In some agencies the people on the sales call are not the people running your account.
  3. Whose ad accounts are they? Insist the ad accounts, pixels, analytics and domains are in your name. Leaving should not mean losing your data.
  4. Can I see work for a business like mine, with permission to contact that client?
  5. How do you report? Monthly reports should show the target, the result and what changes next.
  6. What is the minimum term, and what is the notice period?
  7. What is included in the fee, and what costs extra: creative, ad spend, software, revisions?
  8. What will you need from me each week? Agencies stall waiting for approvals and assets.
  9. What would make you recommend we stop? Honest agencies have an answer.
  10. What happens in the first 30 days?

Warning signs

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed returns.
  • Reluctance to put accounts in your name.
  • Case studies with no client named and no way to verify.
  • Long lock-in contracts before any results.

Specialist or full-service?

A specialist (only paid search, only SEO) is often stronger in its lane. A small full-service team can be better when your ads, content and website need to work together and you would rather have one point of contact. Our list of agencies for small business includes both kinds. One of them is Drifted Marketing, SmallBizWire's publisher, which says on its site that it writes one metric and one date into its contracts and keeps ad spend in the client's own account. Those are exactly the kinds of commitments to ask any agency for.

How do I compare agency pricing models?

  • Monthly retainer. Predictable, suits ongoing work. Make sure the scope is written down so the retainer buys something specific.
  • Project fee. Suits a website, a launch or a one-off campaign. Agree what counts as finished.
  • Percentage of ad spend. Common in paid media. It can reward spending more rather than spending well, so pair it with a performance goal.
  • Performance-based. Attractive in principle, but define the measure carefully and agree how it is tracked.

Whatever the model, ask for the total monthly cost including ad spend, tools and production, not just the fee.

How do I check an agency's claims?

  1. Ask for case studies with the client named, and then ask to speak to that client.
  2. Look at the agency's own marketing. An agency selling content should publish good content.
  3. Check that the people on the sales call are the people who will do the work.
  4. Ask what went wrong on a past project and how they handled it. A candid answer is a good sign.
  5. Be wary of guaranteed rankings or guaranteed returns; nobody controls search engines or markets.

What should happen in the first 90 days?

  • First two weeks: access set up in your name, tracking checked, goal and baseline written down.
  • First month: first campaigns or content live, first report against the goal.
  • By day 90: a clear view of what is working, what is not and what changes next.

If after 90 days you cannot tell whether the work is moving the agreed number, that is the conversation to have, calmly and with the contract in hand.

Agency, freelancer or in-house?

An agency gives you several skills at once. A freelancer can be cheaper and very good for one discipline. An in-house hire gives you full attention but needs managing and a full salary. Many small businesses start with a freelancer or small agency for a defined goal and bring work in-house once they know what works.

For a starting shortlist, see our roundup of marketing agencies for small business. If your marketing depends on email, ask any agency how they handle SPF, DKIM and DMARC and domain reputation; vague answers there are a warning sign.

What should I prepare before talking to agencies?

  • The one number you most want to move, and by when.
  • Your current monthly marketing spend, including ads and tools.
  • A list of the accounts you own: ad platforms, analytics, website, store, email.
  • What you have tried before and roughly how it went.
  • Who in your business will make the decision and approve work.

Sending the same short brief to every agency makes their answers comparable and shortens every first call.

Questions readers ask

What should I ask a marketing agency before hiring?

Ask what they will measure and by when, who will do the work, who owns the ad accounts, what the fee includes, and what the notice period is.

Should my ad accounts be in my name or the agency's?

Yours. Owning the accounts, pixels and analytics means you keep your data if you change agencies.

How much should a small business spend on a marketing agency?

It depends on goals, market and scope. Ask each agency for the full monthly cost including ad spend, tools and production, and compare against what the agreed goal is worth to you.

How long should I give an agency before judging results?

Agree a review point in the contract, often around 90 days for ads and longer for SEO. By then you should at least see clear movement against the agreed measure.

Is a long contract with an agency a bad sign?

Not always, but make sure it includes a notice period and handover steps, and that accounts and data stay yours if you leave.
