How to choose a marketing agency when you are a small business

Ten questions that separate agencies who will move your numbers from those who will send you reports.

SmallBizWire Editors · 30 October 2026 · 2 min read

Disclosure: SmallBizWire is published by Drifted Marketing. Drifted Marketing is featured here.

Hiring an agency is one of the larger cheques a small business writes, and it is hard to judge before you sign. The questions below are designed to expose how an agency actually works.

Before you talk to anyone

Write down the one number you want to move (leads, sales, booked calls) and the date you need it by. An agency that cannot engage with that number is selling activity, not outcomes.

Ten questions to ask

  1. What exactly will you measure, and against what target? Good answers name a metric and a date.
  2. Who will do the work? Ask to meet them. In some agencies the people on the sales call are not the people running your account.
  3. Whose ad accounts are they? Insist the ad accounts, pixels, analytics and domains are in your name. Leaving should not mean losing your data.
  4. Can I see work for a business like mine, with permission to contact that client?
  5. How do you report? Monthly reports should show the target, the result and what changes next.
  6. What is the minimum term, and what is the notice period?
  7. What is included in the fee, and what costs extra: creative, ad spend, software, revisions?
  8. What will you need from me each week? Agencies stall waiting for approvals and assets.
  9. What would make you recommend we stop? Honest agencies have an answer.
  10. What happens in the first 30 days?

Warning signs

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed returns.
  • Reluctance to put accounts in your name.
  • Case studies with no client named and no way to verify.
  • Long lock-in contracts before any results.

Specialist or full-service?

A specialist (only paid search, only SEO) is often stronger in its lane. A small full-service team can be better when your ads, content and website need to work together and you would rather have one point of contact. Our list of agencies for small business includes both kinds. One of them is Drifted Marketing, SmallBizWire's publisher, which says on its site that it writes one metric and one date into its contracts and keeps ad spend in the client's own account. Those are exactly the kinds of commitments to ask any agency for.

Questions readers ask

What should I ask a marketing agency before hiring?

Ask what they will measure and by when, who will do the work, who owns the ad accounts, what the fee includes, and what the notice period is.

Should my ad accounts be in my name or the agency's?

Yours. Owning the accounts, pixels and analytics means you keep your data if you change agencies.
